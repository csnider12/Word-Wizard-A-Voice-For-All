# Appendix: Broader Impacts

| Category | Benefits | Risks and Tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|
| **People — Public Health, Safety & Welfare** | Shock-absorbent TPU bumper reduces injury risk when dropped or thrown. USB-C eliminates the need for proprietary cables. Simple NFC tap input reduces cognitive load for the intended user. | Easy-access screws for repair can be a safety hazard to young/curious individuals. TPU bumper increases overall device footprint. Drawer system requires the device to have two separate parts to manage. |
| **People — Global / Cultural Factors** | Color-coded Fitzgerald Key is a widely recognized AAC standard. The Piper TTS model allows the system to output multiple languages and dialects. | Color coding may not transfer across different cultures. NFC programming requires a smartphone or other proprietary device. |
| **Planet — Environmental** | Programmable cards reduce the need for laminate/print waste. Modular electronic hardware extends device life, reducing waste. Rechargeable battery reduces single-use waste. ABS and TPU are recyclable materials. | Components like the PCB are non-biodegradable. Current manufacturing process is energy-intensive. |
| **Profit — Economic** | Lower cost than tablet-based AAC alternatives. Design is easily scalable and modular for large-quantity school/care-center needs. Electronics are easy to fabricate, and per-unit price drops with bulk orders. | Switching to SPI protocol would require additional R&D man-hours, raising costs. Long product lifespan reduces repeat business unless a compelling updated product is released. |

## Ecodesign Strategies Considered

- **Optimize end-of-life system** by making all hardware components accessible via common screws, increasing ease of disassembly and recyclability — tradeoff: could tempt users to tinker and harm device functionality.
- **Optimize initial lifetime** by reducing the number of soldered-to-connector parts, easing repairability — tradeoff: could reduce reliability if parts get jostled loose during use.
- **Reduce materials** by further consolidating the electronics footprint to shrink the device and reduce material use — tradeoff: could increase electronics complexity and cost.

The team believes there is a viable middle ground across these strategies that balances repairability, reliability, and cost.
